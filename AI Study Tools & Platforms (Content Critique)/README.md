#### 2. Critical Reflection on Tool Limitations

While the AI platform consolidated three dense policy documents into clear thematic summaries within seconds — a task that would have taken a research assistant several hours of manual reading — it consistently displayed two failure patterns worth flagging for the Research Director:

1. **Scope conflation:** The tool repeatedly blurred *national* statistics into *regional* (Mindanao-specific) claims, likely because the source documents discuss Mindanao within a national survey context without isolating regional figures. Any AI-summarized regional statistic must now be manually traced back to its disaggregated source table before being trusted.
2. **Directional fabrication:** In one case, the AI did not simply omit nuance — it asserted a conclusion **opposite** to what the cited paper actually found (claiming a "closed" digital gap when the source explicitly documents a persistent one). This is the more dangerous failure mode, since it reads as confidently sourced rather than vague.

**Verification note:** The PIDS row was initially flagged using a search-result snippet rather than the source PDF. It has since been re-checked directly against the original document (§3.4.2 and §3.4.4), and the correction above reflects that direct read — including the fact that the "most excluded" and "involuntary exclusion" findings come from two separate analyses in the paper, not one combined statement.

**Policy implication:** No AI-generated regional statistic enters a published brief without being checked against the primary source's actual disaggregated data — national-level framing is not an acceptable proxy for Mindanao-specific claims.

#### 3. Vetted Findings Carried Forward to Policy Brief

- National account ownership rose from 29% (2019) to 56% (2021), per BSP survey data — but no verified Mindanao-specific account-ownership figure exists in the sources reviewed; this is flagged as a **data gap**, not assumed to mirror the national trend.
- Digital retail transaction share nationally reached 57.4% by 2024, surpassing the BSP's 50% target — confirmed, but cannot be cited as a Mindanao-specific achievement.
- Per the PIDS paper's account-usage analysis, Mindanao registers as the most financially excluded region nationwide, particularly in fintech/mobile-money usage (§3.4.2).
- Per the same paper's analysis of barriers to account ownership, financial exclusion in the Philippines is found to be largely involuntary, with such barriers greatest outside the National Capital Region — especially in Visayas and Mindanao (§3.4.4).
- These two findings should anchor the policy brief as distinct, source-verified points — not as a single quote, and not as the AI's fabricated "closed gap" narrative.
